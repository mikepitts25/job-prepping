P10. Ten-Week Plan and the Free Material

A schedule you can actually keep, and an honest list of what is free, what is cheap, and what is worth paying for.

Before week 1

Do these in this order. They take an evening.

  1. Join PMI (~$139 with the application fee). This pays for itself against the exam fee alone and unlocks the free standards library.
  2. Download the free ECO and skim it whole. Twenty pages. Do not study it yet — just see the shape of the thing.
  3. Download the PMBOK Guide 8th edition and the Agile Practice Guide from your member benefits.
  4. Choose and buy your 35-contact-hour course. This is mandatory, so it is not optional spend. See the options below.
  5. Draft your application experience descriptions and message your verifiers now, while you are still motivated.

The ten weeks

Assume 6–8 hours a week: roughly one weekday evening plus one weekend morning. Adjust the calendar, keep the sequence.

Week Focus Output
1 35-hour course, first third. Read P1 and P2. Application submitted.
2 Course, second third. Read P3 twice. Skim P4. The seven rules, from memory.
3 Course, final third. Read the Scrum Guide end to end. 35-hour certificate in hand. Book the exam for week 11.
4 P5: scope, WBS, schedule, critical path, estimating. Work a network diagram and a float calculation by hand.
5 P5: earned value, forecasting, quality, procurement. All EVM formulas recalled cold. Do the worked example without looking.
6 P6: conflict modes, Tuckman, motivation, leadership, communication. First mock exam. A baseline score, and a weak-domain list.
7 P7: Manifesto, Scrum, Kanban, hybrid tailoring. First pass of P9. Agile and predictive answers no longer blur.
8 Business Environment: P2's translation table, then only the deltas — opportunity responses, escalate, sustainability, AI governance (P4). Should be your fastest week. Lean on the CISSP.
9 P8 drills, then 200+ practice questions. Grade on reasoning. Second pass of P9. The four recurring error causes, written down.
10 Second full mock. Target 75%+. Drill weak areas only. Weak areas closed, not just identified.
11 Third full mock under real conditions: 240 minutes, two breaks, no interruptions. Third pass of P9 — marked misses only. Then sit it.

Three rules about this plan:

Book the exam in week 3. Not week 9. An open-ended study plan expands indefinitely, and the sunk cost of a booked date is the most effective motivational device available.

Mock exams from week 6, not week 10. A mock is a diagnostic, not a final. The first one should be uncomfortably early and uncomfortably bad. Its job is to direct weeks 7–10. Do at least two full 200-question mocks in the final two weeks, each in one four-hour sitting — the endurance is a separate skill from the knowledge, and it is the one people neglect.

Do not read PMBOK cover to cover. It is a reference. Use it to look things up when a drill exposes a gap. People who try to read it linearly lose two weeks and retain very little, because it is not written as a course.

Pacing on the day

240 minutes, 180 questions, two 10-minute breaks. That is 80 seconds per question including reading time.

  • The case-study section comes first and is timed separately. Read the narrative once, carefully, then answer its questions — you cannot return to a section after you leave it.
  • Take both breaks. Every time. The last hour of a four-hour exam is where fatigue converts knowledge into careless errors, and a break is the cheapest countermeasure there is.
  • Mark and move on. If a question is taking more than two minutes, flag it and go. Time lost on one hard question costs you two easy ones later.
  • Long scenarios often have one decisive sentence. Find the stem question first, then read the narrative looking for it.
  • Answer every single question. There is no penalty for a wrong answer and there is a guaranteed zero for a blank one. Never leave one empty.
  • Flag anything you are unsure of and come back. Later questions frequently supply a term, a definition, or a framing that unlocks an earlier one.

Pacing checkpoints

Work to checkpoints rather than watching the clock continuously. With 180 questions in 240 minutes you have about 80 seconds each, so:

After You should have used no more than
60 questions ~80 minutes
120 questions ~160 minutes
180 questions 240 minutes

If you are ahead at a checkpoint, slow down slightly and read more carefully. If you are behind, start flagging and moving faster — running out of time is far more costly than any single hard question.

The physical side

Not trivial on a four-hour exam. Eat a real breakfast with protein, be properly hydrated, and use the bathroom before you start. Fatigue in the final hour is what converts knowledge into careless errors, and the two scheduled breaks exist precisely to counter it. Take both.

The free material, ranked

Free and essential

Resource Why
PMP Examination Content Outline (PDF) The syllabus. Free, 20 pages, definitive. Read it three times over ten weeks.
The new PMP exam (PMI) PMI's own summary of the July 2026 changes.
PMI Certification Handbook Eligibility, application, audit, and exam policy. Read before applying.
The Scrum Guide Free, ~15 pages, and the authority for every Scrum question.
PMI Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct Short, and directly examinable.

Free with PMI membership (~$139/year)

  • A Guide to the Project Management Body of Knowledge (PMBOK Guide), 8th ed.
  • Agile Practice Guide
  • Process Groups: A Practice Guide — the predictive process mechanics that PMBOK 7 and 8 deliberately leave out. If you find PMBOK 8 too abstract for the Process domain, this is the missing book.
  • The Standard for Risk Management in Portfolios, Programs, and Projects
  • PMI's digital library and webinars, which also earn PDUs later.

Worth paying for

Resource Roughly Verdict
35-contact-hour course $50–$500 Mandatory. Cheap online providers meet the requirement perfectly well; the certificate is what PMI checks.
PMI Study Hall ~$59–$100 The best single purchase. PMI's own simulator, built against the current outline, with explanations. Nothing third-party matches it for question realism.
A prep book ~$40 Optional. Useful if you want a linear narrative; make sure it is the 2026 edition, because the reweighting invalidates older books' study allocations.

Check the date on anything you already own. The July 2026 change was substantial enough to invalidate study plans built on older material. A quick test: if a deck says the exam is 230 minutes, it predates July 2026 — the current exam is 240 minutes with two 10-minute breaks. Likewise, material built around the 2021 ECO's 8% Business Environment weighting will badly under-allocate your time on what is now a quarter of the paper. Older material is not useless — the underlying project management is stable, and the People and Process content ages well — but treat its exam-format and weighting claims as out of date and check them against the current ECO.

Be careful with free question banks scraped from older exams. A large share of the free PMP questions circulating online are written against the 2021 outline or earlier, and some are against the pre-2021 one. Questions built around the old 8% Business Environment weighting, or around ITTO memorisation, will train the wrong instincts. If a free bank asks you to recall inputs and outputs of a process, it is out of date.

PDUs, for afterwards

60 PDUs every three years, across education and giving back, with minimums in each of the PMI Talent Triangle areas. PMI membership includes enough free webinars to cover most of it, and you will find that many activities you already do — including the CISSP CPE-earning ones — qualify for PMP PDUs too. Log them as you go rather than in the last month of the cycle.

If you have less than ten weeks

Compress like this, not proportionally:

  1. Read P3 until the seven rules are reflex. Non-negotiable.
  2. Read P2 and the real ECO.
  3. Memorise the EVM formulas in P5. Cheapest marks.
  4. Work P9 three times. Second cheapest marks.
  5. Read P6 — it is a third of the exam and the least intuitive for you.
  6. Read the Scrum Guide.
  7. Do 300 realistic practice questions, grading on reasoning.

That is the 80/20. The thing you cut is the linear reading, never the questions.
